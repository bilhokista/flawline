Fill this in **after** the problem, customer and offer stages, never before. A
model written first is a wish list with a grid drawn around it.

The third column is the part most versions of this exercise leave out, and it is
the part that makes the page honest. Copy the confidence from the matching claim
in `strategy/`, so that a box resting on an assumption cannot present itself as
settled fact.

| Box | Your answer | Claim it rests on | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## How to read a filled-in page

- **Every box at `assumed`.** Normal on day one. It is a hypothesis, and saying
  so out loud is the only thing that keeps it useful.
- **Some boxes settled, one critical box assumed.** That assumed box is where
  the next two weeks of work belong. Everything downstream of it is provisional
  whether or not it looks confident.
- **Every box confident, no evidence cited.** The most dangerous version. A page
  like this has usually been filled in from reasoning, and it will be defended
  for months.

Run `npx thesis-os status` to see the confidences rather than transcribing them
from memory.

## Why the claim column exists

A one-page model is a summary, and summaries lose the thing that matters most:
whether any of it is known. Pointing each box at a claim id keeps the page tied
to the evidence underneath it, so that filling the page in cannot quietly
upgrade a guess into a fact.
