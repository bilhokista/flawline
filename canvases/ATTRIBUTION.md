# Attribution, lineage and licence terms

This directory is licensed separately from the rest of the repository, and the
separation is deliberate rather than bureaucratic. Read this before reproducing,
renaming or adapting anything in it.

- **Code** (`packages/`) is MIT. See [../LICENSE](../LICENSE).
- **This directory** (`canvases/`) is Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike
  3.0 Unported, because it contains an adaptation of works released under that
  licence, and ShareAlike requires adaptations to carry it forward. See
  [LICENSE](LICENSE).

Keeping them apart means you can vendor, fork or embed the code without
inheriting a copyleft obligation, and it means the licence the original authors
chose is actually honoured rather than quietly dropped.

## What this project builds on

### Business Model Canvas — Strategyzer AG

Designed by Alexander Osterwalder and Strategyzer AG. Released under
**Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 Unported**.

Adaptation is permitted, provided the original source is credited and the
adaptation carries the same licence. Strategyzer additionally ask, as good
practice, that you contact them before including it in software.

<https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas>

### Lean Canvas — Ash Maurya

Adapted by Ash Maurya from the Business Model Canvas, and likewise released
under **Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 Unported** with an explicit
invitation to share and remix.

[one-page-model.md](one-page-model.md) in this directory is an adaptation of the
Lean Canvas. That is the file the ShareAlike obligation attaches to.

<https://leanstack.com/lean-canvas>

### Value Proposition Canvas — Strategyzer AG

**Not** Creative Commons. It is copyright Strategyzer AG, and its stated terms
are more restrictive than those of the Business Model Canvas: it may not be
adapted, included in software, or sold without the express permission and
agreement of Strategyzer AG.

**This project therefore does not reproduce, adapt or embed it, and does not use
its name for any feature.**

The `offer` stage addresses the same territory — matching what a customer is
trying to get done against what an offer does about it — using its own structure
and its own language, because the underlying ideas are not anyone's property but
that particular expression of them is. If you want the Value Proposition Canvas
itself, get it from Strategyzer, on their terms:

<https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-value-proposition-canvas>

### Jobs to be done

The idea that customers buy progress rather than products is long-standing work
associated with Clayton Christensen, Tony Ulwick and Bob Moesta, among others.
It is a body of thought, not a licensed artefact. The `customer` stage owes it a
clear debt and uses plain language rather than any one author's terminology.

## If you are unsure

Two rules cover almost every case.

1. **Reusing the code?** MIT. Take it, no obligation beyond the notice.
2. **Reusing, adapting or redistributing anything in `canvases/`?** Credit the
   original authors as above, and release your adaptation under CC BY-SA 3.0.

If you intend to build a commercial product that reproduces a Strategyzer tool
rather than referencing it, contact Strategyzer first. That is their stated
request for the Business Model Canvas and their requirement for the Value
Proposition Canvas. This project is not a licence to skip that conversation.

## Corrections welcome

If any characterisation above is wrong — including from the rights holders
themselves — open an issue and it will be corrected quickly. Getting this right
matters more to the project than any individual template does.
