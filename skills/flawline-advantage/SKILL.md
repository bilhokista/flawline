# Advantage

Owns `strategy/advantage.md` and the claims `what-is-authentically-mine`,
`context-values-it`, `expensive-to-fake`, `combination-is-mine`. The model
stage's `moat-or-honest-absence` depends on `expensive-to-fake` directly.

Read [../DISCIPLINE.md](../DISCIPLINE.md) before writing anything.

## Why this runs second

An advantage found at the end is a justification assembled to fit decisions
already taken. Found early, it decides which problem is worth taking on, which
segment will pay for this founder specifically, and which channel they can
sustain when a better-funded competitor arrives.

If the founder wants to skip it, say once that they will otherwise choose a
segment that merely tolerates them, and then respect their decision.

## Refuse the list of strengths

The founder will offer strengths. Write them down, then set them aside and say
why: strengths are mostly purchased. A trained skill, a certificate, a tool, a
technology — anything purchasable can be purchased by someone with more money.

Then ask the question that does the work:

> **What is true of you that you would not have chosen?**

Push through the first three answers, which will be modest-sounding strengths in
disguise. Look for four kinds of material:

- **Constraints that cannot be removed.** Where they live, what they can afford,
  how much time exists, who will not return their calls.
- **Traits that have cost them something.** Impatience, obsessiveness, an
  inability to let a wrong number go, discomfort in rooms where everyone agrees.
- **History that does not fit.** The wrong degree, the failed venture, the
  unrelated decade, the thing left off the profile.
- **Taste nobody asked for.** The detail they cannot stop noticing, the standard
  they hold when no one is watching.

Record these unpolished, in their words. A sentence that has already been
reframed cannot be reframed usefully a second time.

## Then find the arena, not the affirmation

The mechanism is not that defects are secretly virtues. It is that a trait has
no fixed value: the same characteristic is penalised in one setting and
rewarded in another, so the work is choosing the setting.

Eric Barker calls these intensifiers in *Barking Up the Wrong Tree*, arguing
from differential susceptibility research — traits normally treated as
liabilities become disproportionate advantages in the right environment. The
conclusion is uncomfortable and practical: the task is not self-improvement, it
is to stop competing in an arena that charges them for who they are.

For each item, three questions:

1. **Where does this get punished?** Specifically. That is the arena to leave.
2. **Where would it be the exact thing required?** That is `context-values-it`.
3. **Who is already in that arena?** Nobody — ask why. Everybody — then it is
   table stakes there, not an edge.

Do not accept a general answer. "Businesses that value quality" is not an
arena. "Clients who have already been burned by a cheap contractor and will pay
to not repeat it" is.

## Run the forgery test, and let it fail

This is the claim that separates an advantage from a personality quirk, and the
one founders avoid because it can come back negative.

Ask literally: **what would it cost a competitor to fake this credibly?**

| Cost to fake | What they have |
| --- | --- |
| A weekend | Nothing. A preference. |
| A hire | A feature. It gets copied once it is proven. |
| A year of doing it badly in public | A real advantage, for roughly a year. |
| Years of experience they did not live | An advantage that compounds. |
| They would have to become someone else | The only kind that lasts. |

Say the row out loud and name it. Most things founders call an unfair advantage
sit on row two — a feature with a hiring cost attached. That is not a reason to
drop it. It is a reason to know how long they have, which is information they
can act on.

The bottom two rows are only reachable for things that are authentically theirs.
A competitor can buy a skill, hire a specialist, license a technology. They
cannot buy having been the person who lived it, and taste that came from lived
experience gets found out when it is imitated.

## Look for the combination

Dave Trott's argument in *One Plus One Equals Three* is that ideas are
recombinations, and that the useful connections come from material the person
was not naturally drawn to. Most people accumulate more of what they already
know, which yields more of what already exists.

So ask what two **worlds** they hold together that nobody else in this market
does — not two skills. The trade they learned before this one, a community they
belong to for unrelated reasons, a second language, a craft with no connection
to the business.

A combination resists copying better than either half, because a competitor
would have to want both, and almost nobody wants both.

## Accept "nothing yet"

If nothing survives the forgery test, write `what-is-authentically-mine` as
well as it can be written and leave the rest `assumed`. Do not manufacture an
advantage to fill the document — a confident sentence nobody believes is worse
than an honest gap, and this tool exists to stop exactly that.

Then tell them what the gap means, plainly: they are competing on execution
alone, in an arena anyone with funding can enter behind them. That may be an
acceptable position. It should be a decision rather than a discovery.

## Evidence rules specific to this stage

This is the one stage where the founder is a legitimate primary source, and the
limits are exact.

- `method: self-report` caps at `indicated`, and only on this stage. They know
  their own history; they are not a reliable judge of its worth, having
  rehearsed their own explanation of it for years. Carried into a stage whose
  claims are about other people, the same evidence drops to `assumed`.
- `context-values-it` needs outside evidence: somebody chose them, paid them, or
  approached them **for this reason specifically**. Record `won-for-this-reason`.
- A deal lost for a missing quality is evidence too. Record
  `lost-for-this-reason`. It maps where the trait is *not* an asset, which is
  more useful than another testimonial.
- `expensive-to-fake` is settled by competitor behaviour: someone tried to copy
  it and produced something worse, or nobody tried despite obvious incentive.
  Record `competitor-failed-to-copy`.

"People always tell me I'm good at this" is a self-report in a borrowed coat.
Compliments and encouragement buy nothing here, as everywhere.

## Handoff

Carry `context-values-it` into `flawline-customer` and `flawline-offer`: choose
a segment that pays for the oddity rather than tolerating it, and build the
offer only this founder would think of.

Carry `expensive-to-fake` into `flawline-model`, where
`moat-or-honest-absence` depends on it. A moat claim with nothing settled here
is a moat claim with nothing underneath it, and the checker will say so.
