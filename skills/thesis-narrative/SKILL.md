# Narrative

Owns `strategy/narrative.md` and the claims `why-this-exists`,
`positioning-is-contested`, `story-is-theirs-not-yours`.

Read [../DISCIPLINE.md](../DISCIPLINE.md) before writing anything.

## Vision and mission are different objects

- **Vision** is the world once you have won. No product in it. Test: would you
  still want it if a competitor caused it? If not, it is a business goal wearing
  a vision's clothes.
- **Mission** is your assignment inside that world. Product allowed. Test: could
  an observer tell whether you did it today?

Most drafts fail the same two ways: the vision contains the product, and the
mission contains an adjective. Cut both and rewrite.

## Positioning, in a form that can be wrong

> For **[segment]** who **[trigger]**, **[name]** is the **[category]** that
> **[the one thing]**, instead of **[the alternative they use today]**.

Every bracket must trace to an earlier stage:

- `[segment]` from `segment-is-reachable`
- `[trigger]` from the interview transcripts
- `[the one thing]` from the single strongest row of the offer table
- `[the alternative]` from `they-already-try`

If `[the alternative]` is empty, stop and go back. A position with no opponent
has nothing to displace, and a customer with no opponent has no reason to move.
This is why `positioning-is-contested` is named the way it is: a position that
nobody could disagree with is a description.

Use the customer's words, from the transcripts, especially any phrase that
appeared in three separate conversations. Your category name matters less than
whether they recognise themselves in the sentence.

## The story, where the customer changes

Four moves, in order, with the customer as the subject of all four.

1. **The world as they live it.** Recognition. They should feel seen, not sold
   to. Use the specific detail from a transcript, not the general truth.
2. **What it quietly costs them.** Small and concrete beats sweeping. The
   Monday that disappears, not "lost productivity."
3. **What becomes possible.** The change, in their terms and their units.
4. **What to do next.** One action, unmistakable.

Your product appears briefly in move three. If it appears in move one, you have
written a brochure. If the founder's own journey appears anywhere, cut it — it
belongs on an About page, not in the story that has to do work.

## Brand is downstream, always

Voice, look, name and typography are consequences of the position. Decide the
position first and the brand then has something to be consistent with. Reversed,
companies produce beautiful assets that say nothing, at considerable expense,
and then wonder why the assets did not help.

If a founder wants to discuss visual identity before `positioning-is-contested`
is settled, say once why the order matters. If they still want to, help them —
but leave the claim marked `assumed`.

## The only test that counts here

Narrative claims feel true, which makes them the easiest claims in the whole
thesis to over-mark. Resist it.

Mark `positioning-is-contested` as `indicated` only when **someone repeated your
position back to you accurately, unprompted.** Not "they said it sounded great."
Retelling. If they cannot retell it, the sentence has not landed, however much
everyone likes it.

Record the attempts in the prose: who you said it to, what they said back, and
where it got garbled. The garbling shows you which clause is doing no work.

## Watch for these

- **Vision inflation.** "Transforming how the world works" is unfalsifiable and
  therefore useless as a decision aid.
- **Category invention too early.** Naming a new category is expensive and only
  works from a position of strength. Early on, borrow a category they already
  understand and win the modifier.
- **Adjective stacking.** Simple, powerful, intuitive, seamless. Every competitor
  claims all four, so none of them distinguish you.
- **Copy written before the interviews.** It will be made of the founder's words
  and will sound like every other company in the space.

## Handoff

Move to `thesis-motion` once someone outside the company can retell the
position. Selling a position nobody can repeat means re-explaining the company
on every single call, forever.
